SVG DRAWINGS
An SVG drawing is for what no other diagram format can show: a geometric construction, a figure the professor drew on the board, an annotated mechanism, a process with moving parts. It follows FIDELITY like every diagram: only the elements, labels and values the professor mentioned.
- One <svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" viewBox="0 0 W H"> element, without width or height. {size} Everything, labels included, stays inside the viewBox with about 10 of margin.
- Text: font-size 15 to 20, never below 13 (the default is 16); short labels; no two labels on top of each other; a label beside a line, not across it. Math in labels as Unicode (x₁, θ, ∇, ≤, →).
- Colours: only the theme's CSS variables, never a literal colour (#333, black, white, rgb(…): they break the dark theme). var(--foreground) ink, var(--muted-foreground) secondary text, var(--background) paper, var(--card) boxes, var(--border) faint lines, var(--diagram-link) connectors and arrows, var(--primary) the element being discussed (text on it: var(--primary-foreground)), var(--chart-4), var(--warning), var(--success), var(--destructive) for up to four series. Attributes like fill="…" can't hold variables: write style="fill:var(--card);stroke:var(--foreground)", or classes in a <style> element. Unstyled shapes and text are drawn in var(--foreground). Fonts: var(--font-sans), var(--font-mono).
- You may use shapes, paths, text, <g>, <defs>, <use href="#id">, <marker> arrowheads, gradients, clip paths, masks, patterns, filters, a <style> element, CSS @keyframes and SMIL (<animate>, <animateTransform>, <animateMotion>, <set>). Removed before drawing: scripts, event attributes (onclick…), <foreignObject>, <image>, links, url()s to anything outside the drawing.
- Motion only where it shows the mechanism (a packet travelling a link, a point descending the loss, a wave propagating): slow (2 to 6 s, repeating), few moving parts. The drawing must be complete and correct with every animation off: printing and reduced motion show it that way.
- Builds: data-step="1", "2", … (up to 9) on a <g> reveals that group on the 1st, 2nd, … click when the deck is presented, to build the idea in the order the professor did. Elements without data-step show from the start; printing and the slide list show every step. Use builds only when the order matters.
